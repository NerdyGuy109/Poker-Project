Flop runout cheat sheet

Exact turn and river odds for every kind of flop · Texas hold’em (and any game with a flop, turn and river)

How to read it. Turn odds count the 49 cards not on the flop; river odds (“by the river”) count all 1,176 possible turn + river pairs. The board is all that’s known: hole cards aren’t removed, so each relevant card in your own hand moves a turn number by about 2%.

Suit and pairing odds are the same for every flop of that type, which is what makes them worth memorizing. Straight odds depend on the ranks, so they’re given as averages with a range.

Counting rule for two connecting cards

With exactly two flop cards that could share a straight, in the middle of the deck, the turn makes a straight possible with 4 cards for each rank that fits: 24 cards when they touch, then 4 fewer for each gap.

Memory tips

  • Count cards, then double: on the turn each card is about 2% (exactly 2.04%). 9 cards ≈ 18%, 11 ≈ 22%, 12 ≈ 24.5%, 36 ≈ 73.5%.
  • The board pairs by the river about 39% of the time (38.8%) on any unpaired flop, roughly 2 times in 5.
  • Two-tone flops bring a flush-possible board by the river 45.8% of the time, nearly half; rainbow flops only 16.8% (runner-runner), about 1 in 6.
  • Monotone flops: the turn is a fourth flush card 1 time in 5 (20.4%); by the river a fourth (or fifth) flush card lands 37% of the time.
  • Paired flops: the case card (trips) arrives 4.1% on the turn and 8.3% by the river; the board becomes two pair 6.1% on the turn.
